Case Study

Apple Music

Role

Product Designer

Timeline

2 weeks

Team

Solo Project

Problem

Like all music streaming platforms, Apple Music is a service that is used by millions of people around the world. People genuinely love the platform and what it has to offer but there are aspects of it that can be enhanced. The goal was to recreate something that was easier and more seamless to use and provided that sense of improvement sought out by some of their most beloved users.

Solution

I reimagined the desktop version For You section which is supposed to recommend personalized music. I focused on the core experience and visual interface.

1. Keeping it consistent with iOS
2. Revamped Layout
3. Redone Artwork
4. Multitasking

Introduction

Music is Life

I religiously listen to music, all kinds too. The focal point of this project was to enhance features, add new ones and improve usability overall to make the service we love even better. I use these music platforms daily, and like many other avid users, I have clear ideas about what we’d love to see on our favorite platforms.

User Interviews

Understanding the Power User

I conducted targeted interviews with four "heavy" Apple Music users. My goal was to identify friction points within the current interface and uncover unmet needs for future-state features.

Competitive Analysis

Benchmarking Spotify & YouTube Music

I analyzed the market leaders to identify gaps in the desktop and mobile streaming experience.

Key Research Takeaways

Static Experience: Competitors lack robust multitasking capabilities that allow users to customize their browsing flow.

Platform Disconnect: While layouts are generally consistent, there is still a noticeable "cognitive load" when switching between mobile and desktop versions.

Problem Definition

Solving for Personalization & Consistency

The primary challenge isn't just aesthetics; it’s how users feel while navigating. My research highlighted a lack of intuitive personalization and cross-platform consistency. I focused this redesign on bridging these two gaps to create a more cohesive, flexible ecosystem.

Ideation

Collaborative Brainstorming

I invited two people to participate in a brainstorming session and at the end of it we were able to decide on the most important aspects to focus on.

Solution

Narrowing the Scope

I prioritized features that provide the highest user value. I narrowed my focus to four key integrations designed to reimagine Apple Music as a more dynamic platform.

User Flow

Integrating New Features

The challenge was introducing a "Sidebar Multitasking" feature without disrupting Apple’s established design patterns. I mapped this user flow to ensure that adding a playlist or album felt like a natural extension of the existing OS experience.

Design Language

iOS Continuity

Great design often lives in the details. To ensure a seamless transition between devices, I mirrored Apple's mobile design patterns:

1. Typography: Utilizing San Francisco to maintain a cohesive visual hierarchy.

2. Adaptive Menus: A concise structure that prioritizes content while retaining mobile navigation logic.

3. Depth & Blur: Implementing the signature iOS "layered" glassmorphism for better visual hierarchy.

4. Dynamic Player: Adopting the iOS 14 color-sampling background blur to keep the visual identity intact.

Revamped Player

Maximizing Utility

The goal was to transform the player from a static bar into a functional hub. I prioritized touch-friendly elements and improved visual hierarchy. A key functional update included making artist names interactive, allowing users to jump directly to artist pages—a critical missing link in the original UI.

Revamped Now Playing

Simplifying the Core Experience

Visual Redesign

Reimagining Album Artwork

“Album Artwork should be treated as part of the UI, not a standalone visual component.” I standardized text placement and aesthetic consistency across all artwork. This ensures users can identify playlists at a glance, removing the guesswork caused by textless or inconsistent graphics.

Feature Spotlight: Multitasking

Non-Interruptive Browsing

Currently, selecting an album interrupts your journey by forcing a page change. My solution introduces a Multitasking Sidebar. Clicking a playlist now opens a persistent side panel, allowing users to view content, manage queues, and browse multiple items simultaneously without losing their place.

Learnings & Outcome

Lessons in System Design

This project was constructed over a span of two weeks and it took some time to precisely come up with the problems that I was going to tackle. This project in particular taught me how to work within the constraints of an existing product and design within an existing system. Since users are already familiar with these conventions, breaking them could hurt usability. When building upon such a huge platform, user testing is essential at every design update to ensure the design still aligns with user experience and business goals. It also opens up more points of focus and use cases to consider so that is something I would want to work on more if I were to do this again. As this was a passion project, I hope it sheds some light on a new possible angle of Apple Music.
