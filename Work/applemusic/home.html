Case Study

Apple Music

Role

Product Designer

Timeline

2 weeks

Team

Solo Project

Despite its global popularity, Apple Music’s desktop experience often feels disconnected from the seamless mobile interface users love. To address this, I reimagined the "For You" section by aligning its visual identity with iOS through redone artwork and a revamped layout. By focusing on a more intuitive core experience and enhanced multitasking, this redesign transforms personalized music discovery into a smoother, more cohesive process for desktop users.

Introduction

Music is Life

I religiously listen to music, all kinds too. The focal point of this project was to enhance features, add new ones and improve usability overall to make the service we love even better. I use these music platforms daily, and like many other avid users, I have clear ideas about what we’d love to see on our favorite platforms.

User Interviews

Understanding the Power User

I conducted targeted interviews with four "heavy" Apple Music users. My goal was to identify friction points within the current interface and uncover unmet needs for future-state features.

Competitive Analysis

Spotify & YouTube Music

I analyzed the market leaders to identify gaps in the desktop and mobile streaming experience. Both offer a largely static experience, lacking robust multitasking capabilities that allow users to customize their browsing flow, and although their layouts are generally consistent across devices, there remains a noticeable cognitive load when switching between mobile and desktop versions.

Problem Definition

Personalization Meets Consistency

The primary challenge isn't just aesthetics; it’s how users feel while navigating. My research highlighted a lack of intuitive personalization and cross-platform consistency. I focused this redesign on bridging these two gaps to create a more cohesive, flexible ecosystem.

Ideation

Collaborative Brainstorming

I invited two people to participate in a brainstorming session and at the end of it we were able to decide on the most important aspects to focus on.

Solution

Narrowing the Scope

I prioritized features that provide the highest user value. I narrowed my focus to four key integrations designed to reimagine Apple Music as a more dynamic platform.

User Flow

Integrating New Features

The challenge was introducing a "Sidebar Multitasking" feature without disrupting Apple’s established design patterns. I mapped this user flow to ensure that adding a playlist or album felt like a natural extension of the existing OS experience.

Revamped Player

Maximizing Utility

The goal was to replace a cluttered, edge-to-edge desktop layout with a focused "island" aesthetic that significantly reduces cognitive load. By utilizing a rounded capsule shape, it creates a clear visual hierarchy that groups playback, track info, and utility controls into logical clusters rather than spreading them across a horizontal bar that wastes space. Using the white space to make the interface feel less like a utility tool and more like a deliberate, premium object that scales effortlessly across different screen sizes. Also, implemented the signature iOS-inspired layered glassmorphism effect with depth and blur to create a clearer visual hierarchy, while also adopting the iOS 14 dynamic color-sampling background blur for the music player to preserve and enhance the app’s visual identity.

Revamped Now Playing

Simplifying the Core Experience

Visual Redesign

Reimagining Album Artwork

“Album Artwork should be treated as part of the UI, not a standalone visual component.” I standardized text placement and aesthetic consistency across all artwork. This ensures users can identify playlists at a glance, removing the guesswork caused by textless or inconsistent graphics.

Feature Spotlight: Multitasking

Non-Interruptive Browsing

Currently, selecting an album interrupts your journey by forcing a page change. So I introduced a Multitasking Sidebar. Clicking a playlist now opens a persistent side panel, allowing users to view content, manage queues, and browse multiple items simultaneously without losing their place. The Now Playing screen is also updated to reflect the mobile UI and new sidebar functionality.

Learnings & Outcome

Lessons in System Design

This project was constructed over a span of two weeks and it took some time to precisely come up with the problems that I was going to tackle. This project in particular taught me how to work within the constraints of an existing product and design within an existing system. Since users are already familiar with these conventions, breaking them could hurt usability.

When building upon such a huge platform, user testing is essential at every design update to ensure the design still aligns with user experience and business goals. It also opens up more points of focus and use cases to consider so that is something I would want to work on more if I were to do this again. As this was a passion project, I hope it sheds some light on a new possible angle of Apple Music.
